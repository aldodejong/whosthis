# Who's This? — Setup guide

This guide is for family and friends who are setting up the app on an iPhone for someone who has Alzheimer's. Following it takes about 20 minutes, mostly for adding photos.

The app helps the person recognise up to five people. When it is sure who is in front of the camera, it shows the person's name and relationship, with a familiar photo, and can say it aloud. When it is not sure, it shows nothing.

You need an iPhone with iOS 17 or later, and the Safari app.

## 1. Open the app in Safari

Open **Safari** on the iPhone and go to:

**https://aldodejong.github.io/whosthis/**

Use Safari for this step. Other browsers cannot install the app to the Home Screen.

## 2. Add it to the Home Screen

1. Tap the **Share** button (the square with an arrow pointing up). It is at the bottom of the screen, or at the top on an iPad.
2. Scroll down and tap **Add to Home Screen**.
3. Tap **Add**.

The Who's This? icon now appears on the Home Screen. From now on, open the app from that icon, not from Safari.

## 3. First open: set a PIN and add people

1. Open **Who's This?** from the Home Screen. Tap **Tap to start**. Allow camera access when asked.
2. To open the caregiver area, **press and hold the small gear icon marked "3s" in the top-left corner of the camera screen for 3 seconds**. The screen changes to the caregiver area.
3. Set a **PIN** of 4 to 6 digits. Write it down and keep it somewhere safe. The PIN stops the person from opening the caregiver area by accident. It is not strong security.
4. Tap **Add a person** and enter:
   - the **name**, for example "Jane",
   - the **relationship**, exactly as you want it said, for example "your daughter",
   - a **display photo**, the one the person sees on screen.
5. Add **3 to 10 enrolment photos** of that one person. Each photo is checked. The app tells you in plain English if a photo is rejected, for example because there is no face, more than one face, the face is too small, or the photo is blurry.

Tips for good photos:

- Use clear, front-facing photos. Good light and a plain background help.
- Only one person in each photo.
- Use a variety of photos: different light, different days, with and without glasses, with different hairstyles. The more variety, the better.
- **Children** and people whose appearance changes (for example, with age, weight, or a new haircut) need more photos, with a wide spread of ages and looks. Expect to add more over time.
- Avoid group photos, sunglasses, hats that cover the forehead, and very dark or very bright photos.

You can add up to five people. Add or change people any time from the caregiver area.

## 4. Turn up the volume and check the silent switch

- Turn the phone's **volume up** using the buttons on the side.
- Check the **silent switch** on the left side of the phone, above the volume buttons. If it shows orange, the phone is on silent, and the spoken name may not be heard.
- Test it: point the camera at the person, or at a photo of them, and listen for "This is ... your daughter" (or whatever relationship you entered).

Speech can be turned on or off in the caregiver area, under **Settings**.

## 5. Lock the phone to the app (Guided Access)

Guided Access stops the person from leaving the app by accident. It is set up once, on the iPhone:

1. Open **Settings**, then **Accessibility**, then **Guided Access**, and turn it on.
2. Tap **Passcode Settings**, then **Set Guided Access Passcode**. Choose a passcode that you will remember. Keep it private from the person.
3. Open **Who's This?** from the Home Screen.
4. Triple-click the **side button** (or the **Home button** on older iPhones) and tap **Start**. The phone now stays in the app.
5. To leave, triple-click again and enter the passcode.

The same steps are in the **Help** page of the caregiver area.

## 6. Privacy

- **Everything stays on this iPhone.** Photos and face fingerprints are stored only on the phone. Nothing about the people or the photos is uploaded anywhere.
- The app needs an internet connection the first time it opens, and when it updates. After that, it works without one.
- **Deleting Safari website data deletes the people.** To do that, go to Settings, then Safari, then Advanced, then Website Data. Removing the app from the Home Screen also deletes the people. If that happens, add them again from the caregiver area.
- Keep the phone and its passcode private.

## 7. What the app does when it is not sure

- **The app shows nothing when it is unsure.** It does not guess. A wrong name is worse than no name, so an unknown or unclear face gets no name.
- If a face has been in view for a few seconds but is not recognised, a small line appears at the top of the screen: **"Not sure who this is"**.
- If the face is too small, the line says **"Please come a little closer"**.
- The line goes away as soon as a person is recognised, or when the face leaves the camera.

## 8. Troubleshooting

- **The camera does not show.** Go to Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Camera, and turn on **Safari** (or the app). Then close the app fully and open it again.
- **"Tap to start" comes back by itself.** The camera was stopped, for example by another app or a phone call. Tap it again.
- **Nothing is spoken.** Turn the volume up, check the silent switch, and check that speech is on in caregiver mode.
- **The person is never recognised.** Add more varied photos (3 to 10 is the limit, so swap in better ones). Check the lighting and that the face is facing the camera and fairly close.
- **The wrong or no name appears.** Let the caregiver check the photos and add better ones. Do not add photos of other people to the same person.
- **A photo is rejected.** Read the reason shown. Usually it is a blurry photo, more than one face, or the face is too small. Choose a clearer photo.
- **The people have disappeared.** Safari website data was probably deleted, or the app was removed from the Home Screen. Add the people again.
- **The app looks out of date after an update.** Close the app fully (swipe it up from the app switcher), then open it again. If it still looks old, open it once with a good connection and try again.
- **The PIN is locked.** After 5 wrong tries, entry is paused for 30 seconds. Wait, then try again.
- **Forgotten PIN.** The app has no way to reset it. The PIN can be changed from the caregiver area's Settings only if you still know the current PIN. Otherwise, deleting the app's data (see section 6) clears the PIN and the people, and you would set up the app again.

If something else goes wrong, note what was on the screen and what you tapped, and send it to the person who looks after the app.
